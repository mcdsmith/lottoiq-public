*Why merging 6/49 and Ontario 49 history is a mistake, even though both games use the same 1–49 pool.*

It's a natural question. Lotto 6/49 and Ontario 49 both draw six numbers from 1 to 49, so it's tempting to think: why not pool every draw from both games and get twice the history to work with?

More data usually means a clearer picture, right?

Not here. The two games share a number range, but they don't share a draw. Every 6/49 draw and every Ontario 49 draw is a separate, independent event, different draw machine, different draw schedule, different balls in the air. The fact that both happen to number their balls 1 through 49 is a coincidence of format, not a sign they're the same underlying process.

**Why merging distorts the numbers.**

Combine the histories and three things go wrong at once:

1. **Frequency counts get inflated.** A number that's genuinely "hot" in 6/49 but ordinary in Ontario 49 will look hotter than it really is in either game, because you've added draws that have nothing to do with 6/49's actual pattern.
2. **Overdue tracking breaks.** The Overdue Numbers feature works by counting draws since a number last appeared in that specific game. If a number just came up in Ontario 49, merging histories would make it look "recently seen" for 6/49 purposes too, even though 6/49 hasn't drawn it in months. That's not a small rounding error; it's a wrong answer dressed up as data.
3. **Pair co-occurrence becomes meaningless.** Two numbers appearing together across a blended history tells you nothing about how often they actually appear together in real 6/49 draws or real Ontario 49 draws. It's a statistic about a game that doesn't exist.

**A simple way to think about it.**

It's the same reason you wouldn't average two different coins' flip histories to predict either coin. Both coins land heads or tails, same "pool" of outcomes, but each coin's results only tell you about that coin. Shared format isn't shared history.

**Why this matters for how you use LottoIQ**

LottoIQ keeps every game's dataset separate for exactly this reason. Lotto 6/49, Lotto MAX, Lottario, and Ontario 49 each get their own frequency counts, hot/cold lists, overdue rankings, and pair statistics, drawn only from that game's own draw history. It's a little more restraint than a "bigger dataset" pitch would offer, but it's the only way the numbers stay honest.

That's the whole philosophy behind LottoIQ: real historical draws, presented exactly as they happened, no shortcuts that make the data look more powerful than it is.
