*Numbers don't just matter individually, their patterns do as well.*

When most people think about lottery analytics, they think about individual numbers, which ones come up most, which ones have gone quiet. But there's another layer worth paying attention to: the structural patterns of winning draws themselves.

The Pattern Statistics section on LottoIQ breaks down the characteristics of recent draws across four dimensions, giving you a clearer picture of what a typical winning set actually looks like.

The four patterns tracked:

1. **Average draw sum:** Add up all the numbers in a typical winning set and you get the draw sum. LottoIQ tracks the average across your selected dataset so you know whether winning draws tend to cluster around lower, middle, or higher totals for that game.
2. **Odd/Even split:** How often does a winning draw contain 3 odd and 3 even numbers versus a heavier split like 4/2 or 5/1? The pattern statistics section shows you the most common breakdown so you can factor it into your picks.
3. **Low/High balance:** How often do winning draws lean toward the lower half of the number pool versus the upper half? This varies more than you'd expect across different games and time windows.
4. **Consecutive pairs:** What percentage of draws contain at least one pair of back-to-back numbers, like 14 and 15 appearing together? More common than most players assume.

**Why it matters for the generator**

These same patterns are what the LottoIQ number generator uses when you apply filters. When you set an odd/even balance or a sum range, those filters are calibrated against real historical draw structure, not arbitrary rules. Your picks reflect how draws have actually behaved, not just gut feel.

**Dataset toggle**

Switch between Last 30, Last 90 (Insider), and All-time (Insider) and you'll see how these patterns shift depending on the time window. The all-time odd/even split for a game can look quite different from what's been happening in the last month.
