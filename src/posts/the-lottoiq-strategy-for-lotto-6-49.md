*Lotto 6/49 is the granddaddy of Canadian lotteries. Two draws a week, pool of 49 numbers, pick 6. Simple enough but with over 13.9 million possible combinations, even shaving off the worst choices matters.*

Here's how LottoIQ approaches it.

**The Game in Numbers**

- **Pool:** 1–49
- **Pick:** 6 numbers
- **Draws:** Wednesday & Saturday
- **Two draws a week means roughly 100 draws a year.** That's a solid dataset, enough for the decay weighting in Due Score to work with real signal rather than noise.

**Quick Orientation: What You're Actually Looking At**

Before the framework, a fast refresher on the Stats screen:

**Due Score:** a 0–100 composite ranking for every number, combining four signals — decay-weighted frequency, trend, overdue/recency, and cluster affinity — each worth up to 25 points, with the full breakdown shown per number.

- **Trend tag:** shown next to each score based on whether the number's recent activity is climbing or fading: ↑ Rising, → Stable, or ↓ Cooling
- **Cluster Affinity:** how often a number appears alongside the current top 10 hottest numbers in the same draw, numbers that run with the hot crowd score higher here.
- **Pattern Statistics:** what typical winning draws look like: sum ranges, odd/even split, low/high balance.
- **Top Number Pairs** *(Insider):* which two numbers show up together most often.

None of these predict the next draw; Due Score isn't a forecast, it's four historical lenses combined into one number, with the full breakdown shown so nothing is a black box. What they do is let you pick with your eyes open instead of picking blind.

**The LottoIQ 6/49 Framework**

**Step 1: Open the Stats screen and check Due Score.** You're looking for numbers scoring 70+ with at least two of the four signals firing. A number that's ↑ Rising and overdue is interesting. A number that's ↑ Rising, overdue, and showing strong cluster affinity? That's your shortlist.

**Step 2: Cross-reference with Top Number Pairs.** Due Score tells you which individual numbers look promising. Top Number Pairs tells you which numbers tend to show up together. If two of your Due Score picks are also a top pair, that's a natural combination, keep them together.

**Step 3: Spread your picks across the pool.** With 49 numbers to choose from, avoid clustering all six picks in a narrow range. A rough target: at least one number in each decade (1–9, 10–19, 20–29, 30–39, 40–49). This isn't superstition, it's about not ignoring 80% of the number pool. (Insider's generator has a Spread filter that does this automatically.)

**Step 4: Use Pattern Statistics as a sanity check.** Compare your six picks' sum and odd/even split against what Pattern Statistics shows for typical winning draws. A set that's wildly outside the usual range, with all six numbers under 15, is rare in the historical record. Not impossible, just rare.

**What to Ignore**

Hot streaks feel meaningful but on their own they're not a system. A number that won three draws in a row isn't "due to stop", each draw is independent. What Due Score captures isn't a streak, it's a pattern across time using decay weighting. That's a different, more defensible thing to act on than reading meaning into a short run.
